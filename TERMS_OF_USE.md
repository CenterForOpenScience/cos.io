### 1. INTRODUCTION 
Center for Open Science, Inc. (referred to as "COS", "OSF", "us" "our" and "we") is a nonprofit culture change organization that aims to align scholarly practices with scholarly values. It does so by developing and advancing a systems strategy to increase rigor, transparency, and sharing of research. COS fosters an open and interactive community among open source developers, open science researchers, and the broader scientific community by providing tools, training, and services that support transparent study planning, output and outcome sharing, and collaboration across research communities.

Center for Open Science, Inc. is affiliated with Center for Open Science AISBL, an international nonprofit association organized under Belgian law ("COS AISBL"). Unless a specific Service states otherwise, the Websites and Services, including OSF, are provided by Center for Open Science, Inc.

The COS mission is to increase the openness, integrity, and trustworthiness of research. To meet that objective, COS employs a systems strategy involving technology to make open practices possible and easy, community building to make open practices normative, and stakeholder engagement to make open practices rewarding and required. COS builds, maintains and operates the OSF, a free, open-source infrastructure for helping researchers register study plans, publish preprints through community-run services, and link related data, materials, code, and outcomes wherever they are stored, creating transparent, connected research records. COS also shares education and training materials and coordinates and conducts events through its websites to advance its mission. **Project.** A "Project" is an OSF workflow that will be disabled beginning on November 16, 2026, and is used to describe an individual or collaborative enterprise that also may have embedded within its subproject(s) or "Project components" or "component(s)" of the Project.  On February 19, 2027, all Projects and Components will be archived and made read-only. Projects can be used to share various research outputs, but study plans, protocols, and preregistrations should utilize the Registrations workflow.

**Preprint.** A “Preprint” is used on OSF to describe a publicly shared document and associated metadata. Preprints cannot be deleted by users, but can be withdrawn, leaving behind metadata about the resource. A preprint must represent a research outcome, typically in a static document format.

**Registration.** A “Registration” is an OSF workflow for creating a public, timestamped record of a research plan that has not yet begun, is underway, or that has concluded with disclosure that it is retrospective. This workflow is not to be used for papers or preprints, including those reporting research outcomes, commentaries, proofs, or other theoretical pieces, or any other research outputs (e.g., data) that are not a part of a research plan. A registration is referenced by a DOI and descriptive metadata. Registrations and cannot be deleted by users, but can be withdrawn, leaving behind basic metadata about the resource.  A registration may also be embargoed for up to four years, but its metadata may be publicly available. 

**Outcome Report.** An outcome report is an OSF workflow for reporting the outcomes of a registered study plan. It provides a structured way to report against the plan, describe deviations, link related outputs, and document all outcomes.

**Content.** As used in these Terms of Use, "content" means, but is not limited to, any research, data, text, images, software and software code, data sets, information, or other materials. Content can be shared on OSF, in a COS blog post, through interacting in a COS-hosted webinar or workshop, etc. 

**OSF Service.** An “OSF Service” is a group of content hosted on OSF. Examples include OSF Preprints, OSF Registries, OSF Meetings, OSF Institutions, and branded community-organized services using OSF infrastructure and designated Moderators and moderation procedures.
